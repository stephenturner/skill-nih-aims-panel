## Research focus
Tsiokas works on the molecular cell biology of polycystic kidney disease and the primary cilium. He was among the first to show that the PKD1 and PKD2 gene products physically interact and that PKD2 (polycystin-2, TRPP2) associates with TRPC1, placing the polycystins in the TRP channel world. His lab has since argued that the polycystin complex functions as a WNT receptor-channel: WNT ligands bind the PKD1 extracellular domain and evoke PKD2-dependent currents and Ca2+ influx, and in 2026 the lab reported that PKD1 behaves as an atypical 11-transmembrane G protein-coupled receptor that couples to Gα subunits to suppress cAMP, with PKD2 chaperoning it to the plasma membrane. The second program concerns ciliary length control and disassembly. His group identified NDE1 as a negative regulator of ciliary length, then showed that CDK5 primes NDE1 for destruction by the SCF-FBW7 ubiquitin ligase, linking the cell cycle to cilium length. From there the lab moved to kidney phenotypes: loss of polycystins delays deciliation via the centrosomal integrity (53BP1-USP28-p53) pathway, and kidney-specific Fbxw7 deletion produces a nephronophthisis-like disease in which SOX9-WNT4-driven fibrosis, rather than cyst burden, determines the decline in kidney function. He also has a long-standing TRPC1 / store-operated Ca2+ entry line that reaches into osteoclasts, parathyroid hormone secretion, CRAC channelopathies (STIM1/ORAI1 gain-of-function in Stormorken syndrome), and metabolic phenotypes of Trpc1 null mice.

## Model systems and methods
- Mouse conditional knockouts: kidney tubule segment-specific Fbxw7 deletion (medullary vs proximal), Pkd1 postnatal deletion models, Sox9 heterozygous compound mutants, Trpc1-/-, I-mfa (Mdfi)-/-, double mutants
- Primary cilia cell biology: ciliogenesis and deciliation assays, ciliary length measurement, centriole/centrosome localization, serum starvation and cell-cycle re-entry assays, NIH3T3/BALB3T3 and MEF systems
- Patch clamp electrophysiology (whole-cell and single-channel) of TRPP2, TRPP3, TRPC1 and CRAC/ORAI1 currents; heterologous expression in HEK293, CHO and Xenopus oocytes; Ca2+ imaging
- Protein biochemistry: yeast two-hybrid, co-immunoprecipitation, domain mapping, ubiquitination assays, structure-function chimeras, G protein coupling and cAMP assays
- Zebrafish and Xenopus embryos for ciliary and tubulogenesis phenotypes (Kupffer's vesicle, left-right patterning, pronephros)
- Mesenchymal stem cell differentiation, osteoclast differentiation and bone microCT; parathyroid gland ex vivo secretion assays
- Human genetics collaborations (STIM1 R304W, ORAI1 P245L in Stormorken-like syndromes); kidney histology, fibrosis scoring, kidney function measurement in mouse models
- Single-cell RNA-seq collaborations on immune cells in cystic kidney disease (with Zimmerman, Yoder)

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values mechanism at the level of specific protein-protein interactions and direct functional readouts. His papers pair domain-mapping biochemistry with patch clamp or Ca2+ flux, so he is likely to reward aims that state which molecules interact where, and to be unsatisfied by "pathway X is dysregulated" claims without a direct measurement.
- Values genetic epistasis in vivo. His strongest kidney papers combine conditional knockouts with compound mutants that rescue a phenotype (Fbxw7 plus Sox9 heterozygosity; NDE1 depletion rescuing Fbxw7 loss). He will likely ask whether a proposed mediator has been tested by a rescue or double-mutant experiment, not just by deletion.
- Values separating correlated phenotypes. The nephronophthisis work explicitly dissociates fibrosis from cyst growth as the driver of functional decline, and the FBW7-cilia work dissociates ciliary length from ciliary signaling output. He is likely to push back on aims that assume cyst burden equals kidney function, or that assume longer/shorter cilia necessarily mean more or less signaling.
- Scrutinizes ciliary claims specifically: whether localization to the cilium was actually demonstrated and at what resolution, whether a phenotype is cilium-dependent or cilium-independent, which Cre driver and which tubule segment was targeted, and the timing of induction (postnatal vs adult), since in his field these change the phenotype drastically. He will also note whether injury was used to accelerate cystogenesis.
- Scrutinizes channel and receptor claims: subunit composition and heteromerization, surface versus intracellular pools, trafficking controls, and whether the claimed ligand (for example WNT) was shown to act directly. Given his own GPCR claim for PKD1, he will be attentive to, and probably demanding about, evidence for G protein coupling and cAMP effects.
- As a department chair and COBRE/T32/R25 leader, he is likely to weigh environment, team composition and training/mentoring plans more than a purely bench-focused reviewer would.
- Where he is an outsider: integrative renal physiology and transport (micropuncture, clearance, sodium handling), neural control of blood pressure, dialysis and vascular access, clinical trials and epidemiology. He would read those as a cell biologist, focused on whether the molecular mechanism is nailed down and whether the model system supports the claim.
- Likely questions at the table: "Does the protein actually localize where you say, and is the function cilium-dependent?" "Which Cre, which segment, and when was it induced?" "Is the effect on cysts or on fibrosis and function, and have you separated them?" "Where is the rescue experiment?" "Is the interaction direct?" "Does the human mutation data support the mechanism?"
