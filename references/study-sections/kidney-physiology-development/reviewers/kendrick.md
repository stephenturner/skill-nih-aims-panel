## Research focus
Kendrick is a patient-oriented clinical trialist whose central question is why people with CKD have so much cardiovascular disease and whether modifiable "nontraditional" risk factors (metabolic acidosis, disordered mineral metabolism, vitamin D deficiency, phosphate, uric acid) cause the vascular dysfunction. Her signature design is a single-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in CKD stage 3b-4 (eGFR 15-44) with a physiological vascular endpoint (brachial artery flow-mediated dilation, aortic pulse wave velocity) rather than hard outcomes. Several of her larger trials have been informative negatives (calcitriol vs cholecalciferol; NaHCO3 in normal-bicarbonate CKD), which shapes a skeptical, mechanism-checking reading of efficacy claims. She has extended this program to kidney transplant recipients (alkali therapy, dapagliflozin), to cognition in CKD (NIH Toolbox, transcranial Doppler), and to repurposed drugs (montelukast/leukotriene antagonism). Earlier work used large cohorts and trial biorepositories (HOST, HEMO, MESA, SPRINT, NHANES) for epidemiology of FGF23, vitamin D, bicarbonate and outcomes, and she also has a line on pregnancy outcomes in women with kidney disease, solitary kidney and living donors, plus collaborations on complement biomarkers in AKI.

## Model systems and methods
- Human participants only; no animal or cell work as PI (co-author on one mouse vascular calcification paper, CHOP/ER stress, 2014).
- Single-center double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs and randomized crossover pilots in CKD 3b-4 and kidney transplant recipients (n = 18-128).
- Vascular physiology: brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (endothelium-dependent) and nitroglycerin-mediated dilation (endothelium-independent), carotid-femoral / aortic pulse wave velocity, carotid IMT, LV mass by cardiac imaging; serum calcification propensity (T50).
- Acid-base and mineral metabolism measures: plasma bicarbonate, 24-h urine ammonium, citrate and pH; FGF23 (intact and C-terminal), PTH, 25(OH)D, 1,25(OH)2D, phosphate.
- Cognitive testing (NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery) and cerebrovascular reactivity / MCA pulsatility by transcranial Doppler.
- Secondary analyses of trial cohorts and registries: HOST, HEMO, SPRINT, MESA, ARIC, NHANES; integrated health-system data (Intermountain) with matched cohort designs for pregnancy outcomes.
- Industry-partnered phase 3 and real-world studies (calcimimetics, phosphate binders, potassium binders, tenapanor).
- Biomarker studies in critically ill adults/children (urine complement factor Ba) via collaborators.

PMID 37228030. Her flagship R01 trial (n = 109, CKD 3b-4, normal bicarbonate): NaHCO3 raised bicarbonate and lowered urinary ammonium but did not improve FMD or PWV at 12 months; transient 1-month FMD effect. ## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values: rigorously designed, double-blind, placebo-controlled human trials with pre-specified primary physiological endpoints; demonstration of target engagement (her own trials report urinary ammonium, citrate, bicarbonate, 25(OH)D, PTH to prove the intervention worked); realistic power calculations. Her 2025 cognition paper explicitly called itself underpowered, so she will likely look for honest sample-size justification.
- Values translational relevance to patients: inclusion of women (she has published sex differences in endothelial function and cognition), diverse cohorts, and feasibility data (crossover pilots before R01s).
- Will scrutinize: whether a surrogate endpoint (e.g., FMD, PWV, eGFR slope, biomarker) is validated and whether a positive pilot will replicate; she has seen promising pilots go null in larger trials (bicarbonate, vitamin D). Expect questions about effect-size assumptions borrowed from pilots, durability of effect (her NaHCO3 FMD effect vanished by 6 months), and off-target effects (bicarbonate raising phosphate/FGF23).
- Will scrutinize observational aims for confounding and effect modification (diuretic use in MESA bicarbonate analysis, indication bias in HOST RAAS analysis handled with propensity scores).
- Outsider for: basic developmental biology, nephron progenitors, cilia, genetic model organisms, single-cell or imaging-heavy mechanistic work, transporter biophysics. She would read such applications as a generalist and likely focus on significance, feasibility, human relevance and whether the aims are interdependent.
- Partial overlap: acid-base and potassium handling, mineral metabolism (phosphate, FGF23, vitamin D, PTH), kidney transplant physiology, pregnancy/solitary kidney outcomes, SGLT2 and diabetic kidney physiology (via Bjornstad collaborations on kidney oxygenation and hemodynamics).
- Likely table questions: "What is the primary endpoint and is it powered?" "How will you show the intervention engaged its target?" "Is the pilot effect size realistic?" "How do you handle sex as a biological variable?" "If Aim 1 fails, do Aims 2 and 3 survive?" "What is the path from this mechanism to a trial in patients?"
- As Chair she will run the discussion and summarize; expect her to press reviewers to tie scores to concrete weaknesses rather than to rank topics.
