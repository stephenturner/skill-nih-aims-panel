## Research focus
His program centers on "uremic vascular biology": why the venous segment of hemodialysis arteriovenous fistulas (AVF) and PTFE grafts (AVG) develops neointimal hyperplasia and stenosis, and how uremia affects the vasculature more broadly (CKD-associated cardiovascular disease). He frames the core question as "what happens to the heart and blood vessels of a zebrafish, mouse, pig or human when their kidneys fail?" Current mechanistic work, with Gang Xi, studies vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) phenotypic switching (IRS-1/KLF4 signaling, diabetes) and intrinsic arterial vs. venous SMC differences in response to uremic serum. Alongside this he runs a large translational and clinical portfolio: hemodynamics/wall shear stress and AVF maturation (HFM Study), device and biologic trials for access stenosis and catheter infection, tissue-engineered conduits, cannulation-skill technology, and kidney innovation policy.

## Model systems and methods
- Porcine AV graft and AVF models (femoral/carotid; PTFE graft-vein anastomosis), including uremic pigs and porcine tunneled dialysis catheter models
- Murine AVF models (carotid-jugular end-to-side; collaboration with Leiden group), with micro-CT plus ultrasound-based computational fluid dynamics (CFD) of wall shear stress and oscillatory shear index
- Primary porcine arterial vs. venous VSMCs exposed to uremic serum: proliferation/migration assays, dedifferentiation markers (calponin, PCNA), KLF4, Runx2/ALP calcification markers, Western/IF, bulk RNA-seq with GSEA/ORA pathway analysis
- Human vascular access histopathology (stenotic graft-vein anastomosis tissue; IHC for SMC/myofibroblasts, macrophages, PDGF, bFGF, VEGF, angiogenesis)
- Microfluidic/organ-on-chip uremia models (indoxyl sulfate, monocyte-endothelial interactions; with Polacheck)
- Biomaterials: NO-releasing catheter lock solutions, electrospun regenerative AV grafts, flow-conditioning anastomotic devices (with engineering collaborators)
- Human prospective cohorts with MRI/ultrasound and CFD (HFM Study, n=602), randomized device trials (WAVE), phase 3 drug trials (LOCK IT-100), implantable loop recorder arrhythmia study (MiD)
- Zebrafish is named in his stated research question; no zebrafish paper was found in the PubMed records reviewed

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values: work that is clearly translational, with a line from mechanism to a therapy, device or biomarker for patients with CKD/ESKD. His bios stress "getting new therapies into CKD and ESKD patients," and he led the Kidney Health Initiative. He is likely to reward aims that name a clinical problem with high morbidity and a plausible path to the clinic, and multidisciplinary teams (engineers, imaging, biostatistics), which match his own team science.
- Values: validation across species and scales (cells, mouse, pig, human tissue or cohort). He builds pig and mouse AV models and pairs them with human histology and cohort data, so a mouse-only story claiming clinical relevance to dialysis access will likely draw a request for large-animal or human-tissue corroboration.
- Scrutinizes: whether the uremic milieu is actually modeled. His recent papers stress that uremia changes SMC behavior differently in arteries and veins, so aims studying vascular or kidney-vascular biology in non-uremic animals or cells, or generalizing arterial findings to veins, are vulnerable.
- Scrutinizes: hemodynamic confounders. He works with WSS/OSI CFD and will probably ask whether flow, anastomotic geometry or vein diameter were measured or controlled when attributing remodeling to a molecular pathway. He also attends to diabetes as an effect modifier.
- Scrutinizes (clinical aims): endpoint choice and definitions, such as unassisted vs. assisted maturation, target lesion vs. access circuit primary patency, and adjudicated CRBSI, plus feasibility of recruitment and intervention-discouragement windows, from HFM and device RCT experience.
- Outsider: classic tubular transport physiology, nephron development, ciliopathies/PKD genetics, renal nerves and neural control of blood pressure, ion-channel biophysics. He would read these as an informed clinician-scientist generalist, focused on significance for patients and on translational framing.
- Likely questions at the table: "Is this relevant to the uremic patient, and how is uremia modeled?" "Arterial or venous, and does the mechanism hold in both?" "What is the path from this target to an intervention?" "Has it been shown in a large-animal model or human tissue?" "Is sex/diabetes accounted for?" "Is the team multidisciplinary enough (imaging, engineering, biostatistics)?"
