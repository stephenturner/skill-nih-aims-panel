## Research focus
Stocker is an autonomic and cardiovascular neurophysiologist. His central question is how the brain senses neurohumoral signals, such as plasma and cerebrospinal fluid Na+ concentration, osmolality, angiotensin II, insulin and leptin, plus signals arising in the kidney itself, and converts them into changes in thirst, vasopressin release, sympathetic nerve activity (SNA) and arterial blood pressure. One program line concerns dietary salt: his group has shown that high-salt intake raises CSF Na+, that neurons in the organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis (OVLT) respond to NaCl distinctly from osmolality, that OVLT drive of SNA is routed through the rostral ventrolateral medulla, and that OVLT neurons are hyperresponsive in Dahl salt-sensitive rats. A second line concerns renal sensory (afferent) nerves: what they detect (chemosensitive vs mechanosensitive stimuli, ischemia, renal pelvic pressure), the role of the TRPV1 channel, and how afferent renal signaling contributes to renovascular (2-kidney-1-clip) hypertension, which is the physiological rationale behind renal denervation. He also collaborates on human studies at the University of Delaware (hydration/hypertonic saline protocols, microneurography, functional MRI of Na+-sensing brain regions).

## Model systems and methods
- Rats (Sprague-Dawley; Dahl salt-sensitive and salt-resistant strains) and C57BL/6 mice
- CRISPR/Cas9-generated knockout rats: Trpv1 (26-bp exon 3 deletion) and Agtr1a (11-bp frameshift in exon 3); also Trpv1 knockout mice
- In vivo multifiber recording of renal, splanchnic, lumbar and adrenal sympathetic nerve activity; afferent renal nerve activity recording
- In vivo single-unit recording of OVLT neurons and spinally projecting rostral ventrolateral medulla neurons; in vitro whole-cell patch clamp of OVLT neurons in slices
- Optogenetic and chemogenetic (DREADD) activation or inhibition of OVLT and subfornical organ neurons in conscious animals
- Radiotelemetry blood pressure, ganglionic blockade as an index of neurogenic pressor activity, intracerebroventricular and intracarotid infusions, OVLT microinjection (muscimol, hypertonic NaCl vs equi-osmotic sorbitol/mannitol)
- Renovascular 2-kidney-1-clip model; selective afferent renal denervation (periaxonal capsaicin) vs total denervation (phenol); retrograde tracing from kidney to dorsal root ganglia
- Behavioral measures of thirst and salt appetite; plasma/CSF electrolyte and osmolality measurements; glomerular filtration rate
- Human translational studies with Delaware collaborators: water deprivation protocols, hypertonic saline infusion, ambulatory and beat-to-beat blood pressure, muscle sympathetic nerve activity (microneurography), resting-state fMRI connectivity of SFO/OVLT
- Methodological rigor work: systematic comparison of anesthetics (Inactin, urethane, isoflurane, decerebrate) and of sex and circadian time on afferent and efferent nerve responses

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values causal, multi-level evidence: single-unit or patch-clamp recording of the candidate neurons, a targeted manipulation (optogenetic, chemogenetic, lesion, denervation, knockout) and a measured physiological output such as SNA, blood pressure or intake. Aims that stop at correlation (for example Fos immunolabeling or transcript abundance) will likely be asked for a functional test; his own J Neurosci paper shows Fos mapping and recording-based mapping disagreeing.
- Values methodological transparency about preparation: anesthetic agent, decerebrate vs anesthetized, time of day, and sex. He has published systematic comparisons showing that anesthesia can abolish the responses being studied, so an aims page proposing autonomic recordings without specifying the preparation is an easy target for him.
- Values pharmacological specificity backed by genetics. He showed that capsazepine effects persist in Trpv1 knockout rats, and he has published a null result in mice after a positive one in rats. He is likely to ask whether an inhibitor's effect has been confirmed genetically and whether species or strain generalization is assumed rather than tested.
- Scrutinizes stimulus identity: NaCl vs osmolality vs volume; chemosensitive vs mechanosensitive renal afferent activation. He is likely to press on whether the proposed stimulus is specific and physiologically relevant in magnitude, and whether equi-osmotic or pressure-matched controls are included.
- Scrutinizes claims about "the sympathetic nervous system" as a single entity. His data show renal, splanchnic, lumbar and adrenal SNA moving in different directions to the same stimulus, so an aims page that measures one nerve and generalizes will be questioned.
- Also attentive to whether behavioral drinking or fluid intake is a confounder for blood pressure effects, since he has explicitly clamped intake to separate the two.
- Where he is an outsider: tubular transport biochemistry and channel structure-function, nephron development, polycystic kidney disease genetics, immunology and fibrosis, dialysis access and clinical trial design. He would read those as a generalist physiologist, judging experimental logic, controls and whether the physiological endpoint is meaningful. He does have adjacent exposure through collaborations in kidney injury imaging, bladder biomechanics and distal tubule potassium handling (co-author on a WNK/KS-WNK1 paper).
- Likely questions at the table: "Which nerve or which neurons did you record, and under what anesthesia?" "Is the stimulus specific, and what is the osmotic or pressure control?" "Have you tested both sexes?" "Does the pharmacology hold up in the knockout?" "What is the causal manipulation, and what is the integrated physiological readout?"
