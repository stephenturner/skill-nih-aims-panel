## Research focus
Barr is a C. elegans geneticist who made the worm a model for ADPKD and other ciliopathies. Her postdoctoral Nature paper (1999) identified lov-1, the worm PKD1 homolog, as needed for male mating behavior. Her lab then showed that LOV-1 and PKD-2 act in the same genetic pathway and localize to sensory cilia of male-specific neurons. Her questions are how polycystins and other ciliary membrane proteins are sorted, trafficked to and kept in cilia (IFT, kinesin-3 KLP-6, CIL-1 phosphoinositide phosphatase, STAM/Hrs, CK2/calcineurin), and how cilia become specialized (tubulin isotypes and glutamylation, RFX isoforms, nephrocystin modules). Since 2014 the lab's main theme has been ciliary extracellular vesicles. Worm sensory cilia shed PKD-2-carrying EVs that work in animal communication, and the lab studies EV biogenesis sites, cargo sorting and subtypes (EV-TurboID proximity labeling), and how EV release is regulated. Its newest line is a CRISPR-based C. elegans pipeline for classifying conserved ADPKD missense variants by their effects on protein level, ciliary and EV localization, and sensory function.

## Model systems and methods
- C. elegans, especially male-specific ciliated sensory neurons (ray RnB, hook HOB, head CEM) and male mating behavior assays (response, vulva location); core amphid/phasmid neurons for comparison
- Forward genetic screens (e.g., ciliary localization "Cil" mutants), double-mutant epistasis, cell-specific rescue, heterozygote dominance tests
- CRISPR/Cas9 knock-in of endogenous fluorescent tags (dual-color reporters) instead of overexpression arrays in recent work
- Live imaging: Airyscan super-resolution, time-lapse of EV release, kymography of IFT and membrane-protein transport
- Ultrastructure: serial-section TEM and electron tomography (long collaboration with David Hall, Albert Einstein)
- EV biology: in vivo EV visualization, EV isolation, proteomic profiling, modular EV-TurboID proximity labeling, behavioral bioassays of isolated EVs
- Cell-specific transcriptional profiling of EV-releasing neurons; single-cell RNA-seq and in vivo electrophysiology mentioned in her biosketch
- Comparisons to mammalian polycystins (urinary exosomes, renal primary cilia) come from the literature; her own work is in nematodes

## Representative publications
- Barr MM, Sternberg PW. A polycystic kidney-disease gene homologue required for male mating behaviour in C. elegans. Nature 1999. PMID 10517638. Defining paper: lov-1 (PKD1 homolog) is needed for male sensory behaviors, and PKD-2 is in the same neurons.
- Qin H, Rosenbaum JL, Barr MM. An autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease gene homolog is involved in intraflagellar transport in C. elegans ciliated sensory neurons. Curr Biol 2001. PMID 11301258. Linked osm-5/IFT88/Tg737 (ARPKD) to IFT. Polycystins pile up in stunted cilia when IFT fails.
- Barr MM, DeModena J, Braun D, Nguyen CQ, Hall DH, Sternberg PW. The C. elegans ADPKD gene homologs lov-1 and pkd-2 act in the same pathway. Curr Biol 2001. PMID 11553327. Double mutants are no worse than single mutants, and cilia are ultrastructurally normal. A model of epistasis plus EM.
- Hu J, Bae YK, Knobel KM, Barr MM. Casein kinase II and calcineurin modulate TRPP function and ciliary localization. Mol Biol Cell 2006. PMID 16481400. A phospho-site (PKD-2 S534) controls ciliary localization. The abstract frames PC1/PC2 as a mechanosensitive channel in renal cilia.
- Hu J, Wittekind SG, Barr MM. STAM and Hrs down-regulate ciliary TRP receptors. Mol Biol Cell 2007. PMID 17581863. Endosomal sorting controls how much polycystin is in cilia.
- Bae YK, Kim E, L'Hernault SW, Barr MM. The CIL-1 PI 5-phosphatase localizes TRP polycystins to cilia and activates sperm in C. elegans. Curr Biol 2009. PMID 19781942. A forward genetic screen for ciliary-localization defects. Phosphoinositide balance is needed for TRPP targeting.
- Barr MM. Caenorhabditis elegans as a model to study renal development and disease: sexy cilia. J Am Soc Nephrol 2005. PMID 15647338. Her case to nephrologists for worm models of cystic kidney disease.
- Wang J, Silva M, Haas LA, Morsci NS, Nguyen KC, Hall DH, Barr MM. C. elegans ciliated sensory neurons release extracellular vesicles that function in animal communication. Curr Biol 2014. PMID 24530063. Founding EV paper: PKD-2 and LOV-1 are EV cargo, EVs bud at the ciliary base, release needs IFT and KLP-6, and isolated EVs are bioactive.
- Silva M, Morsci N, Nguyen KCQ, Rizvi A, Rongo C, Hall DH, Barr MM. Cell-specific alpha-tubulin isotype regulates ciliary microtubule ultrastructure, intraflagellar transport, and extracellular vesicle biology. Curr Biol 2017. PMID 28318980. Ciliary specialization through the tubulin code.
- Wang J, Nikonorova IA, Silva M, Walsh JD, Tilton PE, Gu A, Akella JS, Barr MM. Sensory cilia act as a specialized venue for regulated extracellular vesicle biogenesis and signaling. Curr Biol 2021. PMID 34270950. EVs come from the ciliary tip and the ciliary base. Different cargoes (PKD-2, CIL-7) go to different EVs, and males change cargo ratios in response to mates.
- Walsh JD, Wang J, DeHart M, Nikonorova IA, Srinivasan J, Barr MM. Tracking N- and C-termini of C. elegans polycystin-1 reveals their distinct targeting requirements and functions in cilia and extracellular vesicles. PLoS Genet 2022. PMID 36574451. Endogenously tagged LOV-1 is cleaved at the GPS. The NTM is secreted to the ECM, while the CTM goes with PKD-2 to cilia and EVs. LOV-1 and PKD-2 work both together and separately.
- Wang J, Saul J, Nikonorova IA, Nava Cruz C, Power KM, Nguyen KC, Hall DH, Barr MM. Ciliary intrinsic mechanisms regulate dynamic ciliary extracellular vesicle release from sensory neurons. Curr Biol 2024. PMID 38838665. Sustained EV release needs KLP-6 and the transition-zone protein NPHP-4, and it does not need neurotransmission.
- Nikonorova IA, desRanleau E, Jacobs KC, Saul J, Walsh JD, Wang J, Barr MM. Polycystins recruit cargo to distinct ciliary extracellular vesicle subtypes in C. elegans. Nat Commun 2025. PMID 40180912. EV-TurboID finds polycystin-associated cargo (PACL-1, PAMLs, TRAF TRF-1/2), and loading depends on LOV-1.
- Wang J, Nava Cruz C, Walsh JD, desRanleau E, Nikonorova IA, Barr MM. A C. elegans model for functional analysis of conserved ADPKD variants in cilia, extracellular vesicles, and sensory signaling. Genetics 2026. PMID 42441958. PKD-2(C180S), the ortholog of human PC2 C331S, acts as a null: it lowers protein level and blocks ciliary and EV localization of PKD-2 and LOV-1. It is not dominant-negative, and PKD-2 is haplosufficient. Sets up a variant-classification pipeline.

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values genetic rigor: null alleles, double-mutant epistasis, cell-specific rescue and dominance tests in heterozygotes. Her papers lean on these more than on correlation or expression changes. She will likely reward aims that test pathway relationships genetically, not only by co-localization or co-IP.
- Values endogenous-level reporters over overexpression. Her recent work moved to CRISPR knock-in tags and quantifies protein levels in cell bodies, cilia and EVs. An aim that infers ciliary localization from overexpressed tagged polycystins, or from antibodies without knockout validation, would probably get a question from her.
- Scrutinizes extracellular vesicle claims closely: where EVs come from (ciliary tip versus base versus multivesicular body), cargo heterogeneity across EV subtypes, whether an isolated EV prep is pure, and whether a "signal" really comes from EV cargo. Her own standard pairs imaging, isolation, proteomics and a bioassay. An aims page that uses urinary EVs as an ADPKD biomarker or mechanism readout may draw questions on subtype and source.
- Scrutinizes ciliary trafficking and localization claims: base versus axoneme versus tip, transition-zone gating, IFT dependence, and resolution (she uses Airyscan and electron tomography).
- Likely enthusiastic about variant-to-function classification of ADPKD missense variants, mechanisms of polycystin complex assembly, cleavage and trafficking, conserved ciliopathy genes (NPHP, BBS, Joubert), and tool-building in simple organisms. She will probably defend model-organism and conserved-pathway approaches if others call them "not translational."
- Premise on cilia as flow sensors (panel fault line): her older papers and biosketch describe PC1/PC2 as a mechanosensory receptor-channel complex in renal cilia. Her own data are about sensory function and trafficking in neurons, not flow. She will probably be more open than Delling to sensory-cilium signaling premises. But she will still want the specific claim (calcium influx, flow) tested, not assumed.
- Sex as a variable: her lab studies male-specific neurons and sexual dimorphism, so she will probably notice any aim that treats sex lightly. This is an inference from her topic, not from a stated position.
- Outsider areas: mammalian renal physiology (transport, GFR, micropuncture, BP), kidney development and nephron progenitors, transplantation, dialysis, clinical trials, epidemiology and imaging biomarkers. She would read these as a rigorous geneticist and focus on causality, controls and how hypotheses are framed.
- Likely questions at the table: "Is this localization at endogenous levels, and is the antibody validated in a null?" "Have you shown the two genes act in the same pathway genetically?" "Which EV population carries the cargo, and how do you know the prep isn't contaminated?" "Does the variant disrupt stability or trafficking or function, and is it dominant-negative?" "What does the cilium actually sense in your system, and how will you test it rather than assume it?"
