## Research focus
Monu is an integrative renal physiologist focused on single-nephron hemodynamics and the intrinsic feedback systems that set afferent arteriolar tone and glomerular capillary pressure (PGC). Those systems are vasoconstrictor tubuloglomerular feedback (TGF, macula densa/NKCC2) and vasodilator connecting tubule glomerular feedback (CTGF, connecting tubule/ENaC). He asks how these feedbacks are reset or unbalanced in obesity, prediabetes and hyperinsulinemia, after unilateral nephrectomy, under high salt, and in genetic models such as Alms1 knockout. He also asks how the resulting rise in PGC causes glomerular damage and hypertension. His current program tests whether insulin activates DCT2/CNT-initiated afferent arteriolar dilation (CTGF) to drive renal hyperperfusion in obesity, with KLHL3 as a candidate regulator. A newer line examines bariatric surgery and renal cortical perfusion, including renal sympathetic mechanisms and aging. In collaboration with Pablo Ortiz he characterized the Alms1 knockout rat as a model of metabolic syndrome with spontaneous hypertension and enhanced TGF sensitivity.

## Model systems and methods
- Rats are his main species: Sprague-Dawley (high-fat diet, 60% fat), Zucker obese and lean rats, Dahl salt-sensitive and salt-resistant rats, Alms1 knockout on the Dahl SS background, and unilaterally nephrectomized rats. Some mouse work (SCID and C57 in the PhD-era cardiorenal studies; meprin-alpha KO in a collaboration).
- In vivo renal micropuncture: stop-flow pressure (PSF) as an index of PGC, TGF response curves with orthograde loop perfusion (0-40 nl/min), and maximal TGF response. Pharmacological dissection with furosemide (NKCC2), benzamil (ENaC/CTGF), dimethylamiloride (NHE) and combined inhibition to isolate CTGF. Renal interstitial hydrostatic pressure. GFR measurement.
- Confocal immunolocalization (for example, ALMS1 in NOS1+ macula densa cells). Glomerulosclerosis index. Proteinuria and albuminuria. Renal macrophage infiltration and interstitial fibrosis.
- Blood pressure: mean arterial pressure and systolic BP. Echocardiography and vascular function in Alms1 KO rats. Metabolic phenotyping (insulin, leptin, body weight, age-dependent onset).
- Both sexes in the recent Alms1 work: female KO rats lose cardiovascular protection. The 2022 HFD TGF paper is MeSH-tagged for both sexes. Earlier micropuncture studies largely used males.
- Earlier training: heme oxygenase-1 induction (CoPP, SnMP), isolated small-artery myography (renal interlobular and mesenteric), a myocardial infarction model of type-1 cardiorenal syndrome, and oxidative-stress signaling.
- Peptide pharmacology collaborations: Ac-SDKP (anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic) in obese, high-salt rats, and Ac-SDKP release from thymosin-beta4 via meprin-alpha and POP.

## Representative publications
1. Monu SR, Ren Y, Masjoan-Juncos JX, ..., Peterson EL, Carretero OA. Connecting tubule glomerular feedback mediates tubuloglomerular feedback resetting after unilateral nephrectomy. Am J Physiol Renal Physiol 2018 (e-pub 2017). PMID 28424211. First-author micropuncture work: enhanced CTGF explains TGF resetting 24 h after UNx, restored by benzamil.
2. Monu SR, Maheshwari M, Peterson EL, Carretero OA. Role of connecting tubule glomerular feedback in obesity related renal damage. Am J Physiol Renal Physiol 2018. PMID 30303713. Zucker obese rats have attenuated TGF and elevated CTGF, and ENaC blockade normalizes them. Links CTGF to obesity-related PGC elevation.
3. Monu SR, Wang H, Potter DL, Liao TD, Ortiz PA. Decreased tubuloglomerular feedback response in high-fat diet-induced obesity. Am J Physiol Renal Physiol 2022. PMID 35224993. HFD (60% fat) for 4 vs. 16 weeks: attenuated maximal TGF, higher PGC, glomerulosclerosis and hypertension. AJP-Renal "author spotlight".
4. Monu SR, Potter DL, Liao TD, King KN, Ortiz PA. Role of Alström syndrome 1 in the regulation of glomerular hemodynamics. Am J Physiol Renal Physiol 2023. PMID 37560774. ALMS1 is in macula densa cells. ALMS1 KO Dahl SS rats have higher TGF sensitivity and PGC, lower GFR and interstitial pressure, and age-dependent proteinuria and sclerosis.
5. Jaykumar AB, Monu SR, ..., Rhaleb NE, Ortiz PA. Alms1 KO rat: a new model of cardiometabolic syndrome with spontaneous hypertension. Acta Physiol 2026. PMID 41691606 (preprint PMID 39386593). Hyperleptinemia precedes obesity. Females are not protected. Shows eccentric LV remodeling.

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- **Will value:**
  - Direct in vivo measurement of renal hemodynamics: micropuncture, TGF curves, PGC estimates, GFR. He has written that micropuncture is "the gold standard" and that very few labs can do it.
  - Pharmacological dissection with specific transporter or channel blockers to separate overlapping mechanisms, as in his TGF vs. CTGF designs.
  - Time-course designs that separate cause from consequence, such as 4 vs. 16 weeks of HFD or hyperleptinemia before obesity in Alms1 KO.
  - Inclusion of females. His Alms1 work highlights loss of female protection.
  - Rat models of salt sensitivity and obesity, and their physiological relevance to human metabolic kidney disease.
- **Will scrutinize hardest:**
  - Claims about glomerular hyperfiltration, PGC or autoregulation inferred only from whole-kidney GFR, albuminuria or histology, without single-nephron or arteriolar measurements.
  - Blood pressure methods (tail cuff vs. telemetry or catheter MAP) and anesthesia effects on renal hemodynamics.
  - Specificity of pharmacological tools: benzamil and amiloride off-target effects, NHE vs. ENaC contributions.
  - Mouse-only hemodynamic claims when rat micropuncture is the standard (inference).
  - Mechanistic links from transporter regulation (NKCC2, ENaC, ALMS1, KLHL3) to vascular feedback without functional readouts.
  - Diet composition and duration in obesity and salt studies, and the timing of measurements relative to the onset of hypertension.
- **Where he is an outsider:** nephron progenitor and developmental biology, podocyte transcriptional and chromatin biology, single-cell/omics-heavy aims, human genetics, transplant immunology, and epidemiology. He would assess these mainly for feasibility, physiological readouts and rigor rather than domain-specific technical depth.
- **Likely questions at the table:**
  - "How will you measure glomerular hemodynamics directly?"
  - "Is the hyperfiltration due to afferent dilation or efferent constriction, and which feedback is responsible?"
  - "How is blood pressure measured, conscious or anesthetized?"
  - "Are both sexes included and powered?"
  - "Have you separated TGF from CTGF or myogenic contributions pharmacologically?"
  - "Is the diet model clinically relevant and at what timepoint do changes appear?"
