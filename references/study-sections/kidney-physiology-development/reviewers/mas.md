## Research focus
Mas is a translational transplant genomics scientist, not a developmental or classical renal physiologist. Her central question is how donor organ quality and early graft injury, such as deceased-donor AKI, ischemia-reperfusion, DCD/ECD donors and preservation method, determine long-term kidney and liver allograft outcomes. She also asks how to measure these processes molecularly to predict and prevent fibrosis and graft loss. Her work has moved through four phases:
- Pretransplant biopsy microarray and transcriptome prediction models (DGF, eGFR, 24-month function).
- Intragraft and urinary microRNA and DNA-methylation signatures of IF/TA, calcineurin-inhibitor toxicity and HCV recurrence.
- Single-nucleus RNA-seq and imaging mass cytometry of human kidney allograft biopsies. These define epithelial plasticity (injured "mixed tubular" and transitional PT states), fibroblast trajectories and macrophage polarization that separate repair from fibrosis, plus recurrent FSGS.
- Mechanistic immunology collaborations on rapamycin, dendritic-cell metabolism and the gut microbiome-alloimmunity axis.

In parallel she runs a substantial liver transplant program (IRI, steatosis, HCC, HCV, machine perfusion) and has been a leader in the International Liver Transplantation Society (President 2025-26). In recent editorials she argues for biology-grounded, non-overreaching noninvasive biomarkers in kidney transplantation.

## Model systems and methods
- Human kidney allograft tissue: pre-implantation deceased-donor biopsies (cohorts of 92 to 270), protocol and for-cause post-transplant biopsies, and FFPE archival tissue. Multicenter cohorts with Montefiore, Northwestern, Zurich, VCU and UTHSC.
- Single-nucleus RNA-seq from clinical needle biopsies (she published an optimized nuclei-isolation protocol), with cell-cell communication and ligand-receptor analysis and trajectory analysis.
- Imaging mass cytometry for spatial protein validation, with validation in independent cohorts.
- Microarrays and RNA-seq, with penalized logistic regression and risk-score modeling for outcome prediction (training/validation splits, AUROC). Biostatistics with Kellie Archer.
- Epigenomics: genome-wide DNA methylation (CpG arrays) with integrative multi-omics (DNAm + miRNA + mRNA). miRNA profiling in tissue, urinary cell pellets and circulation. Urinary extracellular vesicles. Proteomics.
- scRNA-seq of PBMCs (for example, an operationally tolerant kidney recipient). Tolerance-protocol biopsies (FCRx stem cell chimerism trial).
- Clinical endpoints: DGF, eGFR trajectories, 24-month graft function, IF/TA (Banff), recurrent FSGS, relisting and retransplantation.
- Animal and in vitro work, mostly collaborative: mouse kidney IRI with adoptive transfer of rapamycin- or FTY720-conditioned dendritic cells; murine EVLP lung models; rapamycin lymph-node/microbiome mouse studies; Shroom3 transgenic mice (Menon lab); ischemic epithelial-macrophage co-culture and siRNA knockdown.
- Organ preservation: pulsatile pump vs. cold storage transcriptomics; ex vivo machine perfusion (liver, lung).

## Representative publications
1. Mas VR (first author), ... Pretransplant transcriptome profiles identify among kidneys with delayed graft function those with poorer quality and outcome. Mol Med 2011. PMID 21912807. Preimplantation biopsy gene expression (n=92) against DGF and eGFR. The foundation of her donor-quality program.
2. Scian MJ, ... (Mas VR coauthor). Gene expression changes are associated with loss of kidney graft function and interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy: diagnosis versus prediction. Transplantation 2011. PMID 21242883. Microarray of 101 allograft biopsies. Frames her recurring distinction between diagnostic and predictive biomarkers.
3. Mas VR (first author), ... Reduced expression of inflammatory genes in deceased donor kidneys undergoing pulsatile pump preservation. PLoS One 2012. PMID 22545113. Preservation method (PPP vs. cold storage) and its effect on the ECD kidney transcriptome.
4. Maluf DG, ... (Mas VR coauthor). The urine microRNA profile may help monitor post-transplant renal graft function. Kidney Int 2013/2014. PMID 24025639. Urinary cell-pellet miRNA signature of CAD with IF/TA (191 samples).
5. Maluf DG, ... (Mas VR coauthor). Evaluation of molecular profiles in calcineurin inhibitor toxicity post-kidney transplant: input to chronic allograft dysfunction. Am J Transplant 2014. PMID 24698514. Cross-sectional plus longitudinal design linking a CNIT signature to IF/TA progression.
6. Bontha SV, Maluf DG, Archer KJ, ..., Gallon L, Mas VR. Effects of DNA methylation on progression to interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy in renal allograft biopsies: a multi-omics approach. Am J Transplant 2017. PMID 28556588. Integrative DNAm/miRNA/mRNA analysis. Her epigenetics emphasis.
7. Gallon L, ..., Leventhal JR, Mas VR. Intragraft molecular pathways associated with tolerance induction in renal transplantation. JASN 2018. PMID 29191961. Biopsy transcriptomics in FCRx chimerism-induced tolerance recipients.
8. Archer KJ, Bardhi E, Maluf DG, ..., Mueller TF, Mas VR. Pretransplant kidney transcriptome captures intrinsic donor organ quality and predicts 24-month outcomes. Am J Transplant 2022. PMID 35730259. 270 deceased-donor biopsies with training/validation design, a 55-gene model and a 13-gene + 3-clinical-variable qPCR score (AUROC 0.82). Shows her standard for prediction work.
9. Rousselle TV, ... (Mas VR coauthor). An optimized protocol for single nuclei isolation from clinical biopsies for RNA-seq. Sci Rep 2022. PMID 35701599. Methods enabling snRNA-seq on needle biopsies.
10. McDaniels JM, Shetty AC, ..., Maluf DG, Mas VR. Single nuclei transcriptomics delineates complex immune and kidney cell interactions contributing to kidney allograft fibrosis. Kidney Int 2023. PMID 36863444. About 42,000 nuclei. Defines low vs. high ECM fibrosis states and an injured "MT1" proximal tubule state driving fibrosis, plus donor-macrophage crosstalk. Validated by IMC.
11. Yaffe HC, ..., Mas VR, Akalin E. Impact of deceased-donor acute kidney injury on kidney transplantation. Transplantation 2024. PMID 37990359. A review of donor AKI, discard rates and molecular biomarkers. Bridges AKI biology and organ-utilization policy.
12. Azim S, Rousselle T, ..., Menon M, Maluf DG, Akalin E, Mas VR. Epithelial-immune-stromal interactions define divergent repair and fibrosis pathways after acute kidney injury in human renal transplants. bioRxiv 2025. PMID 40364910. snRNA-seq plus IMC of early post-transplant biopsies stratified by long-term recovery. A transitional PT (tPT4) state, a profibrotic fibroblast lineage and MPhi1 vs. MPhi2 polarization separate recovery from non-recovery. Central to how she would read AKI-repair aims.
13. Gallon L, Zubair H, ..., Maluf DG, Mas VR. Cellular and molecular resolution of focal segmental glomerulosclerosis recurrence in human allografts. bioRxiv 2025. PMID 40364909. snRNA-seq of recurrent FSGS: podocyte loss via metabolic and structural dysregulation, VEGF-A overexpression, and PEC dedifferentiation.
14. Reghuvaran A, ..., Mas V, ..., He JC, Menon MC. Design of precision therapeutics for a CKD risk allele by targeting Shroom3-Rock interaction. Nat Commun 2025. PMID 41469391. A collaborative mechanistic mouse and small-molecule study of a GWAS CKD/allograft fibrosis allele.
15. Wu L, ..., Mas VR, Ma B, Bromberg JS. Rapamycin immunomodulation utilizes time-dependent alterations of lymph node architecture, leukocyte trafficking, and gut microbiome. JCI Insight 2025. PMID 40260917. Her immunosuppression-microbiome collaborations (U01 AI170050 group).
16. Mas VR, Sanchez-Fueyo A, Mannon RB. Signal without overreach: toward a biology-grounded framework for noninvasive biomarkers in kidney transplantation. Am J Transplant 2026. PMID 42155827 (editorial). Also Mas VR, Luo X. Can urinary single-cell transcriptomics reveal new dimensions of allograft biology? AJT 2026. PMID 42392365. Current stated views on biomarker rigor.

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- **Will value:**
  - Human tissue relevance. Most of her work is on human biopsies, so she will favor aims that validate rodent mechanisms in human kidney (snRNA-seq atlases, biopsies, urine).
  - Prediction and biomarker work with independent training and validation cohorts, pre-specified clinical endpoints (eGFR at fixed timepoints, DGF, Banff IF/TA), and a biostatistician on the team. Her 2022 AJT paper and 2026 editorial emphasize "signal without overreach".
  - Multi-omic integration and orthogonal validation of sequencing data at the protein or spatial level (IMC).
  - Cell-state resolution of repair vs. failed repair after AKI: injured or transitional PT states, epithelial-immune-stromal crosstalk, fibroblast lineages.
- **Will scrutinize hardest:**
  - Biomarker claims that conflate diagnosis with prediction, lack an independent validation cohort, or are overfit (many features, few events).
  - Single-cell or single-nucleus claims without adequate sample numbers per group, batch handling, or orthogonal validation. Ligand-receptor inferences presented as mechanism.
  - Translational leaps from rodent IRI or UUO to human transplant or CKD without human data.
  - Clinical endpoint choice and follow-up length for outcome aims.
  - Donor and recipient heterogeneity (age, KDPI, DCD vs. DBD, race/APOL1) as confounders.
- **Where she is an outsider:** classical renal physiology (micropuncture, TGF, transport electrophysiology, BP telemetry), embryonic kidney development and nephron progenitor biology, and ciliopathy and cystic disease. She would read these as a generalist, likely focusing on the human relevance and translational pathway rather than technical physiology. She is also not a mouse geneticist; her animal work is mostly collaborative.
- **Likely questions at the table:**
  - "Is there human validation, ideally in biopsies or single-nucleus data?"
  - "Is the cohort large enough and is there an independent validation set?"
  - "What is the clinical endpoint and when is it measured?"
  - "Does this distinguish adaptive repair from maladaptive (failed) repair?"
  - "How will the transcriptomic findings be confirmed at the protein or spatial level?"
  - "Is the biomarker diagnostic or truly predictive?"
