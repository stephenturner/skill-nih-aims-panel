## Research focus
Rauchman studies how chromatin remodeling complexes and their partner transcription factors set the fate of nephron progenitor cells, and how the same developmental pathways are redeployed during kidney repair. The core of his program is SALL1, the zinc-finger transcription factor mutated in Townes-Brocks syndrome and in nonsyndromic CAKUT. He showed that Sall1 represses transcription by recruiting the NuRD (nucleosome remodeling and deacetylase) complex through a conserved 12-amino-acid motif, that Sall1 maintains progenitor "stemness" by restraining premature differentiation into renal vesicles, that disrupting the Sall1-NuRD interaction accelerates differentiation and specifically costs the loop of Henle, and that Townes-Brocks syndrome is caused by a truncated protein acting dominantly rather than by haploinsufficiency. His group has extended this to Brg1/SWI-SNF in progenitor proliferation and to Chd4/NuRD deletion causing low nephron endowment followed by developmentally programmed FSGS with defective mitochondrial lipid metabolism, a model that connects birth defects to adult CKD. A second arm is epigenetic regulation of injury and repair: he is a senior author on the KPMP human kidney chromatin landscape paper, which defined transcription factor networks (ELF3, KLF6, KLF10 in proximal tubule; NR2F1 in thick ascending limb) governing the health-to-injury transition, and on the 2025 multimodal atlas of resolved versus unresolved repair. Clinically and translationally he works on kidney fibrosis, including small-molecule RGD-integrin antagonists, on identifying new genetic causes of CAKUT by trio whole-exome sequencing of fetuses and newborns with severe antenatal kidney defects, and, with VA colleagues, on CKD progression and AKI epidemiology and care delivery.

## Model systems and methods
- Mouse genetics: Sall1 null, Sall1-deltaZn2-10 and Sall1-TBS truncation knock-ins, a Sall1 knock-in that disrupts the NuRD-binding motif, conditional deletion of Mi2beta/Chd4, Brg1 (Smarca4), and Sall1 in Six2+ cap mesenchyme; Wnt9b overexpression transgenics; Foxd1 and Six2 Cre drivers; conditional Sall1 deletion in female reproductive tract
- Kidney injury models: ischemia-reperfusion, aristolochic acid nephropathy, unilateral ureteral obstruction
- Genomics and epigenomics: ChIP-seq, CUT&RUN, CUT&TAG, Hi-ChIP, single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq and ATAC-seq, 10X multiome, spatial transcriptomics, DNA methylation and histone modification profiling (H3K27ac, H3K4me1, H3K4me3, H3K27me3), CRISPR/Cas9 epigenetic editing
- Primary culture of nephron progenitor cells; advanced imaging; co-immunoprecipitation and HDAC activity assays for complex composition; luciferase reporter and deletion mapping for repression domains
- Human genetics: trio whole-exome sequencing for CAKUT, with zebrafish functional assays (per his WashU research-interests statement)
- Human tissue: KPMP biopsies and reference tissue, cross-species mouse-to-human comparison
- Clinical and health-services research with VA data: registry and EHR cohorts of eGFR decline, a nonrandomized intervention study of early nephrology consultation in hospital-acquired AKI, and a randomized placebo-controlled trial of minocycline for post-CABG AKI
- Pharmacology: peptidomimetic RGD-integrin antagonists (CWHM-12, CWHM-680) delivered by continuous subcutaneous infusion or oral gavage

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- **Will value:** separation-of-function genetics over blunt knockouts. His signature move is a knock-in that disrupts one protein-protein interaction (the Sall1 NuRD-binding motif) or expresses a disease-mimicking truncation, and he has shown repeatedly that a null and a mutant allele give different, sometimes opposite, answers. He will reward applicants who propose an allele that tests the specific mechanism rather than deleting the gene. He also values linking a chromatin or transcription-factor finding to a specific nephron segment and to a quantitative outcome (nephron number, loop of Henle markers THP/NKCC2/AQP1, albuminuria, GFR), and he expects human tissue validation where possible, given his KPMP role. He is unusually bilingual: he runs a chromatin lab, sees CKD patients at the VA, and publishes clinical epidemiology, so he responds to a clear line from mechanism to human disease.
- **Will scrutinize hardest:** (1) haploinsufficiency assumed for a human mutation when a truncated or altered protein might be expressed and act dominantly, which is the central lesson of his Townes-Brocks work; (2) chromatin complex claims resting on knockdown or one subunit without evidence of complex composition, occupancy, and genetic interaction; (3) correlative multi-omic atlases that identify transcription factor networks but do not perturb them (his own KPMP paper went on to knock out ELF3/KLF6/KLF10 and track the resulting trajectory); (4) "developmental pathway reactivated during repair" claims, where he will want evidence that the adult expression is in the right cells and that it is functional rather than a bystander injury signature; (5) fibrosis and TGF-beta targeting that ignores toxicity from global inhibition, and fibrosis endpoints without a reliable biomarker or functional measure (he wrote the review flagging both); (6) nephron-number phenotypes reported without stereology or an explicit counting method.
- **Where he is an outsider:** membrane transport biophysics and electrophysiology, acid-base and electrolyte handling at the channel level, ciliopathy cell biology and cilia imaging, immunology and transplantation, and vascular or hemodynamic physiology. He would read those aims pages as a well-informed nephrologist but not a specialist, and his questions there would tend toward disease relevance and in vivo validation.
- **Likely questions at the table:** "Is this a null allele or a mutant protein, and which one models the human disease?" "Have you shown the complex is actually present and occupying those loci in the relevant cells?" "Will you perturb the regulatory network you identified, or only describe it?" "Does the nephron deficit translate into a functional or long-term phenotype?" "Is there human tissue, ideally KPMP, to anchor this?" "How do you count nephrons?" "What happens postnatally, not just at E14.5?"
