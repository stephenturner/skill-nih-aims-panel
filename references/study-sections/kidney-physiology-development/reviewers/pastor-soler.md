## Research focus
Pastor-Soler is an epithelial transport physiologist and cell biologist. Her central question is how kidney and male reproductive tract epithelia sense their chemical and metabolic environment and reposition transport proteins in response. Her signature system is the vacuolar H+-ATPase (V-ATPase) in collecting duct intercalated cells and epididymal clear cells. She showed that luminal alkaline pH and bicarbonate are sensed by soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC), raising local cAMP, and that PKA then drives V-ATPase accumulation at the apical membrane, while AMPK, the cellular energy sensor, opposes this. She mapped the specific phosphosites: PKA on Ser-175 of the V-ATPase A subunit, AMPK on the same subunit (ATP6V1A), and later showed Aurora kinase A phosphorylating Ser-175 in renal carcinoma cells to drive extracellular acidification and migration. A parallel thread examines AMPK as a broad regulator of tubular transporters (ENaC, Na/K-ATPase, aquaporin-2, KCNQ1 via Nedd4-2, the creatine transporter) and as a therapeutic lever: she has tested metformin, AICAR, and bempedoic acid in models of ADPKD, CKD, hypertension, and sepsis-associated AKI. Other lines include Muc1 as a protector of the HIF-1 and beta-catenin pathways in ischemia-reperfusion injury, AKI biomarkers (IGFBP7, TIMP-2) in human tubule cells, acidosis as a driver of CKD progression, and a stated interest in a novel renal cell carcinoma biomarker. She is a co-author on the USC kidney organoid and nephron progenitor platform papers from the Zhongwei Li and Andrew McMahon groups.

## Model systems and methods
- Ex vivo kidney slices (Stadie-Riggs slicer or vibratome, buffered and CO2-equilibrated), her lab's workhorse for studying transporter trafficking in situ; she has written two methods chapters on it
- In vivo luminal perfusion of the rat epididymis at defined pH, with confocal immunofluorescence of fixed tissue
- Primary and immortalized epithelial cell lines: mpkCCD collecting duct, Clone C intercalated-cell-like, S3 proximal tubule, HK-2 and primary human proximal and distal tubule cells, Caki-2 renal carcinoma, Pkd1-null proximal tubule and IMCD lines, HEK-293T
- Xenopus oocyte two-electrode voltage clamp; short-circuit current in polarized monolayers; biotinylation of surface proteins; radiolabeled uptake
- Phospho-site mapping by mass spectrometry, in vitro kinase assays, 32P in vivo labeling, phosphomimetic and phospho-dead mutants (S175A, S175D), RNAi knockdown
- Confocal and quantitative subcellular-localization imaging; immunoblotting of kidney homogenates and nephron-segment transporter profiling
- Rodent models: rat and mouse ischemia-reperfusion AKI, unilateral ureteral obstruction, subtotal nephrectomy, cecal ligation and puncture sepsis, Dahl salt-sensitive hypertension with telemetry and patch clamp of ENaC, Muc1 knockout, Pkd1 RC/RC and doxycycline-inducible Pkd1 ADPKD models with GFR, blood pressure, BUN, and kidney-weight endpoints
- 3D Matrigel cyst assays; mitochondrial function assays (MitoSOX, MitoTracker, oxygen consumption)
- Collaborative work with kidney organoids and expandable nephron progenitor and ureteric bud progenitor cultures

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- **Will value:** mechanism at the level of a named protein and a named residue. Her own standard is to move from a physiological stimulus (luminal pH, metabolic stress) to a kinase, to a mapped phosphosite, to a transport consequence measured electrically or by flux, with phosphomimetic and phospho-dead mutants as the test. She is methodologically self-aware and writes about model-system limits explicitly, so a candid statement of what an ex vivo slice, a cell line, or an organoid can and cannot show will land well. Sex as a biological variable is more than a checkbox for her: she is a co-author on the Veiras sexual dimorphism paper, and her own ADPKD study reports both sexes and finds a more severe female phenotype. She publishes negative results (metformin/AICAR in Dahl rats), so she is likely to respect honest alternative outcomes in an aims page.
- **Will scrutinize hardest:** subcellular trafficking claims. Expect her to ask how apical accumulation was quantified, whether the antibody is validated, whether total protein versus surface protein was distinguished (biotinylation, not just imaging), and whether tissue was fixed fast enough to avoid artifactual kinase activation. Pharmacology gets similar treatment: metformin and AICAR are not clean AMPK activators, and her own work shows metformin effects persisting without OCT1/2 or AMPK-beta1, so she will question whether a drug's effect is actually on-target. For AMPK or PKA claims she will want the kinase manipulated genetically, not only pharmacologically. Studies that treat intercalated cells as acid-base-only cells, or that isolate them out of their collecting duct context without comment, invite her objection. For ADPKD therapeutics she will ask about model choice (rapid doxycycline-induced versus slowly progressive Pkd1 RC/RC) and whether the effect holds alongside tolvaptan.
- **Where she is an outsider:** developmental genetics and transcription-factor networks (she is a co-author on organoid papers but not a developmental biologist), epidemiology and clinical trials, immunology and transplantation, and genomic methods such as ChIP-seq or scATAC-seq. She would read a nephron-patterning or gene-regulatory-network aims page as an interested generalist, with her questions aimed at whether the developmental finding has a transport or physiological consequence.
- **Likely questions at the table:** "How do you know the protein moved to the membrane rather than simply increased?" "Is the kinase effect direct, and which residue?" "What does the inhibitor hit besides your target?" "Did you do this in both sexes?" "Does the cell line still behave like the native segment?" "What is the functional readout: current, flux, urine chemistry, or just a western blot?" "Is this relevant at physiological luminal pH and bicarbonate?"
