## Research focus
Amaral is a pediatric transplant nephrologist and clinical epidemiologist. Her work asks why children, adolescents and young adults with kidney failure have unequal access to transplantation and worse long-term graft survival, and what can be changed at the patient, center and policy level. Her early, defining papers used USRDS and SRTR registries to document racial/ethnic and poverty-related disparities in preemptive and living donor transplantation and in pediatric graft survival, and to evaluate allocation policy (Share 35, KDPI revisions, multi-organ priority). More recent work covers dialysis facility profit status and pediatric access/mortality, risk-based (KFRE) vs eGFR-threshold waitlisting, rehospitalization after transplant, and social-determinant indices (HOUSES, COI, ADI). In parallel she runs behavioral intervention trials on adolescent medication adherence and the transition from pediatric to adult care, collects patient-reported outcomes (fatigue, sleep, psychological distress) in pediatric CKD, and co-leads noninvasive biomarker and imaging studies of allograft injury (urinary mRNA, metabolomics, MR diffusion tensor imaging).

## Model systems and methods
- Human cohorts only; no animal or bench work.
- National registries: USRDS, SRTR/OPTN (UNOS), CMS ESRD Clinical Performance Measures, Pediatric Health Information System (PHIS) linked to SRTR, PEDSnet, MarketScan claims.
- Multicenter pediatric cohorts: CKiD collaborations, MyKidneyHealth PRO cohort (16 centers), VIRTUUS urinary biomarker cohort, TAKE-IT adherence trial secondary analyses, multinational childhood nephrotic syndrome GWAS consortium (co-author).
- Statistical/epidemiological methods: Cox and Fine-Gray competing-risk models, time-updated exposures, mediation analysis, mixed models clustered by center/provider, linear mixed models of eGFR trajectories, propensity-type adjustment.
- Behavioral RCTs using mHealth (Way to Health platform), bidirectional text messaging, financial incentives / behavioral economics, electronic adherence monitoring, tacrolimus intrapatient variability.
- Qualitative and mixed-methods research (semi-structured interviews, caregiver perspectives, resilience during health-care transition).
- Patient-reported outcomes (PROMIS pediatric measures).
- Noninvasive allograft assessment: urinary cell mRNA signatures, urine metabolomics, MRI DTI (fractional anisotropy) correlated with biopsy and CADI scores.

## Representative publications
1. Amaral S, McCulloch CE, Lin F, Grimes BA, Furth S, Warady B, Brunson C, Siyahian S, Ku E. Association Between Dialysis Facility Ownership and Access to the Waiting List and Transplant in Pediatric Patients With End-stage Kidney Disease in the US. JAMA 2022. PMID 35916847. USRDS (n = 13,333): for-profit facilities linked to slower waitlisting (HR 0.79) and transplant (HR 0.71). Her highest-profile R01-era paper.
2. Ku E, McCulloch CE, ..., Furth SL, Warady BA, Grimes BA, Amaral S. Association Between Dialysis Facility Ownership and Mortality Risk in Children With Kidney Failure. JAMA Pediatr 2023. PMID 37669042. Two-fold higher mortality at for-profit facilities, 67% mediated by lower transplant access. Shows her use of competing risks and mediation.
3. Ku E, Copeland T, ..., Savant JD, Lopez I, Amaral S. Strategies to Guide Preemptive Waitlisting and Equity in Waittime Accrual by Race/Ethnicity. Clin J Am Soc Nephrol 2024. PMID 37930674. A KFRE 25% 2-year risk threshold gives more equitable preemptive waittime than eGFR ≤20.
4. Ku E, Copeland T, ..., Furth SL, ..., Amaral S. Association between estimated time with low GFR and access to transplant among youth with advanced CKD. Pediatr Nephrol 2026. PMID 41940939. Black youth spend less time at eGFR 10-30 (faster progression), which reduces preemptive and living donor transplant; used bedside Schwartz, CKD-EPI 2021 and CKiD U25 equations.
5. Amaral S, Patzer RE, Kutner N, McClellan W. Racial disparities in access to pediatric kidney transplantation since Share 35. J Am Soc Nephrol 2012. PMID 22539831. Policy evaluation: Share 35 attenuated deceased-donor disparities but shifted away from living donation.
6. Patzer RE, Sayed BA, Kutner N, McClellan WM, Amaral S. Racial and ethnic differences in pediatric access to preemptive kidney transplantation in the United States. Am J Transplant 2013. PMID 23731389. Black and Hispanic children far less likely to receive living-donor preemptive transplant.
7. Patzer RE, Mohan S, Kutner N, McClellan WM, Amaral S. Racial and ethnic disparities in pediatric renal allograft survival in the United States. Kidney Int 2015. PMID 25337773. Black recipients had markedly worse 5-year graft survival, with declines before the 3-year Medicare cutoff.
8. Amaral S, Schuchard J, Claes D, ..., Furth S, Forrest CB. Patient-Reported Outcomes Over 24 Months in Pediatric CKD: Findings From the MyKidneyHealth Cohort Study. Am J Kidney Dis 2023. PMID 36889426. 212 children/young adults at 16 centers: high, stable burden of fatigue and distress independent of CKD stage.
9. Benz EG, Godown J, Schaubel D, ..., Amaral S. Association of rehospitalization after pediatric kidney transplantation with kidney function at 1-year posttransplant and long-term allograft failure. Am J Transplant 2025. PMID 41314615. SRTR-PHIS linkage across 36 hospitals; 69% rehospitalized in year 1; associated with lower 1-year eGFR and 10-year graft failure.
10. Kumar J, Contrepois K, Snyder M, ..., Keating B, Amaral S. Design and Methods of the VIRTUUS Study in Children. Transplant Direct 2021. PMID 34805493. Multicenter urinary mRNA/metabolomic biomarker validation for acute cellular rejection and BK nephropathy.
11. Quinn SM, Fernandez H, ..., Ginsburg KR, Amaral S. The role of resilience in healthcare transitions among adolescent kidney transplant recipients. Pediatr Transplant 2019. PMID 31441191. Qualitative: connection to the care team (interdependence) predicted stable transition.
12. Amaral S, McCulloch CE, ..., Ku E. Trends in Living Donation by Race and Ethnicity Among Children With ESRD in the United States, 1995-2015. Transplant Direct 2020. PMID 32766425.
13. Amaral S, Kessler SK, Levy TJ, ..., Levine MH, Levin LS. 18-month outcomes of heterologous bilateral hand transplantation in a child: a case report. Lancet Child Adolesc Health 2017. PMID 30169225. First pediatric bilateral hand transplant (prior kidney recipient); shows her clinical transplant-immunology leadership.
14. Amaral S, Copeland T, McCulloch CE, ..., Ku E. Variation in Living Donor Evaluation and Acceptance across the United States. Kidney360 2026. PMID 42821330. Nine-center donor-candidate cohort (Aim 2 of REACH): ancestry differences in acceptance not explained by center/provider variation.
15. Amaral S, Hwang W, Fivush B, Neu A, Frankenfield D, Furth S. Association of mortality and hospitalization with achievement of adult hemoglobin targets in adolescents maintained on hemodialysis. J Am Soc Nephrol 2006. PMID 16943308. Early registry work questioning whether adult targets apply to adolescents.

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values: clinical and population relevance to children, adolescents and young adults; clear outcome definitions; multicenter samples large enough for subgroup analyses; attention to race/ethnicity, insurance and neighborhood factors; patient-centered outcomes (PROs, treatment burden). Her trials and cohorts are multicenter, so she will likely ask whether a single-center design can generalize.
- Will scrutinize: whether developmental or pediatric claims are tested in pediatric-relevant systems or patients (for example, adult-only data extrapolated to children); how kidney function is measured in children (which eGFR equation, cystatin C, age-appropriate thresholds); whether proposed biomarkers have a validation cohort and reference standard (biopsy), as in VIRTUUS; and confounding and selection bias in any observational human aim (competing risks, time-varying exposures, center effects).
- Will expect a translational line: how a mechanism could change care for children with CKD, CAKUT or transplant, or affect allocation or clinical practice.
- Outsider for: mouse developmental genetics, nephron progenitor and ureteric bud biology, transporter physiology, cilia biology, invertebrate models, imaging and omics pipelines for basic mechanism. She would read these as a generalist and likely weight Significance, the human-disease connection and Approach feasibility (power, timelines, alternative strategies).
- Partial overlap: pediatric CKD and congenital anomalies as a clinical context; nephrotic syndrome/FSGS genetics (co-author on HLA/PRS steroid-response GWAS); urinary biomarkers of tubular and allograft injury.
- Likely table questions: "How does this translate to children with kidney disease?" "Is the sample representative, and are disparities considered?" "What is the validation strategy for the biomarker?" "Are sex and age (developmental stage) handled as biological variables?" "Is the human cohort or registry aim adequately powered and adjusted?"
