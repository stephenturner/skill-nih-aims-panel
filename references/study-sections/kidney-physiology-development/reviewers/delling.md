## Research focus
Delling is an ion-channel physiologist who studies the primary cilium as a calcium-signaling compartment. His questions are: which channels live in the ciliary membrane, what ligands gate them, and how ciliary electrical/calcium signals are decoded into transcriptional or developmental outcomes. With Clapham he showed that cilia are a distinct calcium compartment set by PKD1L1-PKD2L1 channels, and, controversially, that primary cilia are not calcium-responsive flow mechanosensors in kidney epithelium, the embryonic node, or inner-ear kinocilia. His independent lab focuses on the PC-1/PC-2 (polycystin) channel complex in ADPKD: reconstituting the heteromer, identifying the PC-1 N-terminal fragment and a cilia-enriched oxysterol (7beta,27-DHC) as required activators, classifying ADPKD missense variants by trafficking versus channel-function defects, and screening small-molecule polycystin activators. A parallel line uses the mouse embryonic node to study cilia and calcium in left-right patterning, and a new chemogenetic tool (CiCaChem) targets defined-pharmacology cation channels to cilia.

## Model systems and methods
- Ciliary patch-clamp electrophysiology (direct recording from primary cilia, e.g., mIMCD-3 cells) and whole-cell recording of reconstituted PC-1/PC-2 channels in HEK293 cells
- Genetically encoded, cilia-targeted calcium indicators (Arl13b-mCherry-GECO1.2 transgenic mouse; ratiometric ciliary calcium imaging)
- Mouse embryonic node (crown and pit cells) for left-right patterning; kidney thick ascending limb and cultured renal epithelia; inner-ear hair-cell kinocilia; ependymal motile cilia
- Mouse genetics, RNA-seq, biochemistry, structure-guided mutagenesis (with Erhu Cao on polycystin structures)
- Variant-effect bioassays for PKD1 missense variants (ciliary localization, surface trafficking, channel function); low-temperature folding rescue; collaborations with UCSF genomics (Ye, Coyote-Maestas) and cilia biology (Reiter)
- Pharmacology: small-molecule agonist/antagonist discovery for polycystin channels; oxysterol synthesis inhibition
- Earlier: TRP channel biophysics (TRPV3, TRPA1, TRPML1/3 mucolipins, TRPM7), Chlamydomonas flagella (collaboration)

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values: direct, quantitative measurement of channel activity at the right subcellular site (ciliary patch clamp or cilia-targeted ratiometric indicators), and causal tests (structure-guided mutations, genetic and pharmacologic loss of function) rather than correlation. His own papers pair reconstitution with native-cilium recordings.
- Values: falsifiable hypotheses and willingness to overturn dogma; two of his key papers (2016 Nature; 2023 eLife) test and reject widely cited calcium-signaling models.
- Scrutinizes hardest: any aim that assumes primary cilia sense fluid flow through polycystin-mediated calcium influx in kidney tubules. He published that this does not happen under physiological or supraphysiological flow, and he will likely ask what evidence supports the premise.
- Scrutinizes: calcium imaging that cannot separate ciliary from cytoplasmic calcium (non-targeted dyes, slow imaging, phototoxicity or movement artifacts), and claims about polycystin "channel function" based only on expression, localization, or downstream signaling without electrophysiology.
- Scrutinizes: localization claims for PC1/PC2 (cilia versus plasma membrane versus ER), trafficking assays, overexpression artifacts in HEK cells, and whether the native heteromer stoichiometry is preserved.
- Likely enthusiastic about: variant-to-function classification, mechanism-based ADPKD therapeutics, and tool development (chemogenetics, sensors).
- Outsider areas: whole-animal renal physiology (GFR, micropuncture, blood pressure), clinical cohorts, imaging biomarkers, epidemiology, nephron progenitor developmental biology. He would read these as an informed basic scientist and would likely focus on mechanistic rigor and premise.
- Likely questions: "How do you know the signal is ciliary and not cytoplasmic?" "Have you measured channel activity directly?" "Is the flow-sensing premise compatible with published negative data?" "Does the variant disrupt trafficking or gating, and how will you tell?" "Are the reagents (antibodies, overexpression constructs) validated in knockout cells?"
