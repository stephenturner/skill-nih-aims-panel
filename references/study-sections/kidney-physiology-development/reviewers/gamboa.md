## Research focus
Gamboa is a physician-scientist trained in muscle physiology and clinical pharmacology. He studies skeletal muscle mitochondrial dysfunction as a driver of sarcopenia, frailty, and poor physical function in CKD and maintenance hemodialysis. His questions: when in CKD does muscle mitochondrial dysfunction appear, what drives it (uremia, inflammation, oxidative stress, bradykinin/RAAS, metabolic acidosis), and can it be reversed with exercise or mitochondria-targeted drugs and supplements (CoQ10, nicotinamide riboside)? A second line examines intermuscular adipose tissue (IMAT) as a marker of muscle quality linked to inflammation, tissue sodium, and function in CKD, hemodialysis, and lupus. Earlier work covered high-altitude hypoxia physiology (Andean highlanders, chronic mountain sickness, mouse hypoxia models) and clinical pharmacology trials of ACE inhibitors versus ARBs, bradykinin, and inflammation during hemodialysis. At UAB he now collaborates with mineral-metabolism groups (FGF23 from skeletal muscle) and muscle-senescence groups.

## Model systems and methods
- Human mechanistic clinical trials: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover designs (ramipril/valsartan, icatibant, CoQ10, nicotinamide riboside) in hemodialysis and CKD stage 3-5 patients
- In vivo muscle energetics by 31P magnetic resonance spectroscopy (phosphocreatine recovery time constant)
- MRI quantification of intermuscular adipose tissue (calf/thigh); 23Na-MRI tissue sodium (collaborative)
- Vastus lateralis muscle biopsies: mitochondrial volume density (EM stereology), mtDNA copy number, BNIP3, PGC1alpha, DRP1/fission-fusion markers, high-resolution respirometry in permeabilized fibers, RNA-seq
- Stable-isotope muscle protein kinetics (forearm), hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic and hyperglycemic clamps
- Circulating biomarkers: F2-isoprostanes, isofurans, cell-free mitochondrial DNA, cytokines; PBMC/monocyte extracellular flux bioenergetics (Seahorse)
- Physical performance: 6-minute walk, handgrip strength; home-based HIIT and exercise interventions
- Earlier: isolated muscle mitochondria polarography, mouse normobaric hypoxia models, diaphragm vs limb muscle comparisons; EHR/biobank genetic association (APOL1, DARC/ACKR1 in SLE) as a co-author

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values: in vivo human physiology with quantitative mechanistic endpoints (31P-MRS, clamps, isotope kinetics) and randomized, placebo-controlled, often crossover designs. His work follows the Vanderbilt clinical-pharmacology habit of testing mechanism in humans with a pharmacologic probe (icatibant, ramipril versus valsartan).
- Values: linking a cellular readout (mitochondrial respiration, mtDNA, fission markers) to a patient-relevant function (6MWT, handgrip). He will probably look for whether a mechanistic aim connects to a physiologic or functional outcome.
- Scrutinizes: confounding and power in small human studies. He uses propensity weighting and states when secondary analyses are underpowered. Expect questions on sample size, crossover washout, multiplicity, and whether a biomarker is specific to mitochondria (for example, isofurans versus F2-isoprostanes).
- Scrutinizes: claims about "mitochondrial dysfunction" based on a single marker (content versus intrinsic function versus dynamics). He distinguishes mitochondrial volume density, enzyme activity, respiration in permeabilized fibers, and in vivo PCr kinetics, and may ask which one an aims page actually measures.
- Scrutinizes: whether rodent CKD models (adenine diet, 5/6 nephrectomy, Col4a3) reflect human uremic muscle, and whether sex, race, diabetes, and dialysis vintage are handled.
- Outsider areas: nephron development, ciliary biology, PKD genetics, single-cell renal transport and channel biophysics, glomerular podocyte biology. For aims in those areas he will likely judge significance, feasibility, and human relevance rather than technical detail.
- Likely questions: "Is the mitochondrial readout functional, or only a content marker?" "How will you show the intervention engaged its target in humans?" "Is the study powered for the mechanistic outcome, and how are confounders handled?" "Does this connect to physical function or another hard outcome?" "Are kidney-muscle or kidney-systemic crosstalk effects being attributed to the kidney when they could be uremic or inflammatory?"
