## Research focus
Kreidberg is a developmental and molecular kidney biologist. His career rests on two mouse knockouts he made: Wt1, which causes renal agenesis, and alpha3 integrin, which disrupts podocyte foot processes, GBM organization and branching. His lab asks how transcription factors (WT1, FOXC2, the PRC2 component EED) and cell-matrix adhesion receptors (alpha3beta1, alphavbeta5) control nephron progenitor maintenance, nephron number and podocyte identity. A second question is how these same programs are re-deployed when podocytes are injured. Current work treats the podocyte injury response as dynamic transcriptional reprogramming: WT1 and FOXC2 binding to target genes rises early and then collapses late in injury. It also asks how injury-driven shifts in the integrin repertoire (loss of alpha3beta1, gain of alphavbeta5) drive YAP/TAZ nuclear activity and capillary-loop architecture. From about 2010 to 2022 he ran a parallel ADPKD program on c-Met/mTOR, Wnt7a/7b/Pax2, CREB/cAMP and fibronectin in cystogenesis. Other threads are microRNA requirements in nephron progenitors and podocytes (with Jacqueline Ho) and BMP7 control of nephron number and proximal tubule growth.

## Model systems and methods
- Mouse genetics: germline knockouts he made himself (Wt1, Itga3). Conditional alleles (Itga3 fl, Yap fl, Wwtr1/Taz fl, Foxc2, Wt1, Eed, Bmp7, Dicer) crossed to Nphs2-Cre or tamoxifen-inducible Nphs2-iCre (podocyte), Six2-Cre (nephron progenitors), and metanephric-mesenchyme and ureteric-bud Cre lines. Pkd1-null mice and cells.
- Glomerular injury models: Adriamycin (BALB/c and other strains; male mice for ADR), nephrotoxic serum nephritis. Glomerular isolation by Dynabead perfusion.
- Genome-wide chromatin methods: ChIP-chip and later ChIP-seq of WT1 and FOXC2 from embryonic kidney and adult glomeruli. ChIP-qPCR. Histone-mark and chromatin-state analysis with Peter Park's group. RNA-seq.
- Human kidney organoids (with Ryuji Morizane) for WT1 reprogramming. Human biopsy immunostaining with Astrid Weins.
- Embryonic kidney explant/organ culture with morpholino knockdown and electroporation of expression constructs. Kidney explant cystogenesis assays (8-Br-cAMP, c-Met inhibitors, DKK1).
- Cell systems: immortalized mouse podocytes (immortomouse-derived), siRNA, integrin-blocking antibodies (ALULA anti-alphavbeta5), FAK inhibitors. Pkd1-/- kidney epithelial cells. MDCK 3D tubulogenesis (collaborative).
- Imaging and quantification: immunofluorescence, EM, capillary-loop circularity and Mander's colocalization in ImageJ, ACR by DCA analyzer.
- Glycoproteomics (nano-HPLC-MS of alpha3 integrin glycans, collaborative).

## Representative publications
1. Kreidberg JA, Sariola H, ..., Jaenisch R. WT-1 is required for early kidney development. Cell 1993. PMID 8395349. Defining paper: Wt1-null embryos have metanephric blastema apoptosis and no ureteric bud outgrowth. This is the origin of his progenitor/induction focus.
2. Kreidberg JA, Donovan MJ, ..., Jaenisch R. Alpha 3 beta 1 integrin has a crucial role in kidney and lung organogenesis. Development 1996. PMID 8951069. alpha3 knockout gives a disorganized GBM, immature podocyte foot processes and reduced collecting-duct branching. This is the root of his integrin/matrix lens.
3. Gao X, ..., Little MH, Kreidberg JA. Angioblast-mesenchyme induction of early kidney development is mediated by Wt1 and Vegfa. Development 2005. PMID 16291795. Wt1 drives Vegfa, and Flk1+ angioblast signaling maintains Pax2/Gdnf. Shows his comfort with explant electroporation and induction biology.
4. Hartwig S, ..., Fraenkel E, Kreidberg JA. Genomic characterization of Wilms' tumor suppressor 1 targets in nephron progenitor cells during kidney development. Development 2010. PMID 20215353. First genome-wide WT1 ChIP in embryonic kidney (1663 targets), with a morpholino explant model.
5. Ho J, ..., Kreidberg JA. Podocyte-specific loss of functional microRNAs leads to rapid glomerular and tubular injury. JASN 2008. PMID 18832437. Nphs2-Cre Dicer deletion. miRNAs are dispensable for glomerular development but needed for barrier maintenance.
6. Qin S, ..., Zhou J, Kreidberg JA. Failure to ubiquitinate c-Met leads to hyperactivation of mTOR signaling in a mouse model of ADPKD. JCI 2010. PMID 20852388. Mechanistic PKD signaling, with alpha3beta1 sequestering c-Cbl in the Golgi. Includes pharmacological rescue in organ culture.
7. Schumacher VA, ..., Kreidberg JA, ..., Ai X. WT1-dependent sulfatase expression maintains the normal glomerular filtration barrier. JASN 2011. PMID 21719793. WT1 targets Sulf1/2 to regulate VEGFA/FGF2 bioavailability in podocyte-endothelial crosstalk.
8. Kann M, Ettou S, ..., Benzing T, Kreidberg JA. Genome-wide analysis of WT1-controlled gene expression in podocytes reveals key regulatory mechanisms. JASN 2015. PMID 25636411. In vivo podocyte WT1 ChIP-seq plus RNA-seq, defining a WT1/LMX1B/TCF21/FOX/TEAD/MAFB enhancer network.
9. Kann M, ..., Kreidberg JA. WT1 targets Gas1 to maintain nephron progenitor cells by modulating FGF signals. Development 2015. PMID 25804736. Links a direct WT1 target to NPC survival and nephron mass.
10. Zhang L, Ettou S, ..., Park PJ, Kreidberg JA. EED, a member of the polycomb group, is required for nephron differentiation and the maintenance of nephron progenitor cells. Development 2018. PMID 29945864. PRC2/H3K27me3 in nephron progenitors. His epigenetics-of-development work.
11. Ettou S, Jung YL, ..., Morizane R, Park PJ, Kreidberg JA. Epigenetic transcriptional reprogramming by WT1 mediates a repair response during podocyte injury. Science Advances 2020. PMID 32754639. Dynamic WT1 binding and chromatin changes during reparative injury, in mice and human kidney organoids.
12. Taglienti M, Graf D, Schumacher V, Kreidberg JA. Bmp7 drives proximal tubule expansion and determines nephron number in the developing kidney. Development 2022. PMID 35877077. Progenitor-derived Bmp7 sets nephron number (reduced to about one tenth without it) and drives postnatal PT proliferation via Smad1/5/8, p38 and JNK.
13. Huynh-Cong E, ..., Schumacher VA, Kreidberg JA. The integrin repertoire drives YAP-dependent epithelial:stromal interactions during injury of the kidney glomerulus. Nature Communications 2025. PMID 40199893. Podocyte YAP/TAZ deletion compromises the barrier, and loss of alpha3beta1 raises alphavbeta5 and nuclear YAP. Both sexes were used except in ADR experiments.
14. Ettou S, ..., Kretzler M, Weins A, Morizane R, ..., Schumacher VA, Lee D, Kreidberg JA. FOXC2 and WT1 regulate transcriptional reprogramming during the podocyte response to injury. JCI Insight 2026. PMID 42258752. Most recent: FOXC2/WT1 co-binding, and their adult podocyte deletion blunts the injury transcriptional response.
15. Quaggin SE, Kreidberg JA. Development of the renal glomerulus: good neighbors and good fences. Development 2008. PMID 18184729. A widely cited review of podocyte-endothelial-mesangial signaling. Shows how he frames the glomerulus as a multicellular signaling unit.

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- **Will value:**
  - Genetic rigor in the mouse. He built germline knockouts and routinely uses cell-type-specific and inducible deletion (Nphs2-iCre, Six2-Cre). He will expect loss-of-function in the relevant cell type rather than global knockouts or cell-line-only data.
  - Genome-wide target identification tied to function. His WT1 and FOXC2 work pairs ChIP-seq with RNA-seq and conditional deletion, so he will look for direct-target evidence, not correlation.
  - Work that connects development to adult disease: re-use of developmental programs after injury, and nephron number as a disease determinant.
  - Use of human material (organoids, biopsies) to anchor mouse findings, as in his 2020 and 2025 papers.
- **Will scrutinize hardest:**
  - Claims that a transcription factor "drives" a program without ChIP or direct-binding evidence.
  - Cre-driver specificity and timing: podocyte vs. PEC vs. mesangial, and inducible vs. developmental deletion. Developmental compensation.
  - Choice of glomerular injury model. ADR is strain- and sex-dependent; he used BALB/c and males only for ADR. NTS and other models differ. He may ask whether findings generalize across injury types and whether the time course (early vs. late injury) is sampled. His own data show TF binding changes dramatically over the course of injury.
  - Integrin and matrix claims: which heterodimer, ligand specificity, and whether in vitro podocyte-line results (immortalized lines on collagen, laminin-521 or fibronectin) reflect in vivo podocytes.
  - Nephron-number claims without a rigorous counting method, and progenitor-depletion claims without proliferation and apoptosis data.
  - In PKD aims: the specificity of signaling nodes (c-Met, mTOR, Wnt, cAMP/CREB) and whether organ-culture cystogenesis translates to in vivo models.
- **Where he is an outsider:** whole-animal renal physiology and hemodynamics (micropuncture, TGF, blood pressure telemetry), tubular transport physiology, epidemiology and clinical trials, transplant immunology, and human genetics or GWAS. He would read these as a generalist and lean on molecular-mechanism criteria.
- **Likely questions at the table:**
  - "Is this a direct target? Where's the ChIP or binding evidence?"
  - "Is the Cre restricted to the cell you say, and is the phenotype developmental or adult?"
  - "Does the injury model and timepoint capture repair versus progression?"
  - "Have you validated in human tissue or organoids?"
  - "How will nephron number be counted?"
