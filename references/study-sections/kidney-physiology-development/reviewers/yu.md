## Research focus
Yu studies how loss of the TSC1/TSC2 tumor suppressors drives mTORC1-hyperactive tumors. Her main disease is lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM), a female-predominant lung disease, and she also works on TSC kidney lesions (angiomyolipoma, renal cysts and cystadenomas). A central theme is estrogen. With Henske she showed that estradiol promotes survival and lung metastasis of tuberin-null cells, and later that estradiol and mTORC2 together drive prostaglandin biosynthesis (COX-2) and pentose phosphate pathway dependence. A second theme is finding pathways that rapamycin does not shut off, as drug targets: prostaglandins, PARP1, phospholipase A2, acid ceramidase and the sphingolipid pathway, PLK1/S1P, and HOX-PBX survival programs. Recent work uses single-cell and spatial multiomics of human LAM lungs, extracellular vesicles in metastasis, and STAT1/PD-L1 tumor immunity. Her kidney-specific work is mainly collaborative with Manoocher Soleimani (UNM): TSC renal cysts are made of A-intercalated cells, depend on FOXI1 and c-KIT, and may secrete chloride via ClC-5, unlike CFTR-dependent cysts in PKD.

## Model systems and methods
- TSC2-deficient cell models: Eker rat uterine leiomyoma ELT3 cells (with TSC2 re-expression controls), Tsc2-/- MEFs, patient-derived LAM and angiomyolipoma cells. Her lab reports deriving the first LAM-associated patient-derived primary cell line.
- Xenograft and metastasis models in mice: estrogen-induced lung metastasis of tuberin-null cells (her lab calls it the first metastatic model of LAM), circulating tumor cells, short-term lung colonization, IVIS in vivo bioluminescence imaging
- Genetic mouse models: Tsc2+/- mice with spontaneous renal cystadenomas, used for combination-therapy testing. In collaboration, principal-cell-specific Tsc1 knockouts crossed with Foxi1, Car2 or c-Kit knockouts, read out by kidney MRI and histology.
- Hormone manipulation (estradiol, progesterone, fulvestrant/Faslodex, tamoxifen); she uses both male and female mice in metastasis work
- Pharmacology alongside mTOR inhibitors: rapamycin and rapalogs versus Torin 1 and Rictor knockdown to separate mTORC1 from mTORC2; COX-2 inhibitors (celecoxib, aspirin), ASAH1 inhibitor ARN14976 (17a), HXR9 (HOX-PBX blocker), imatinib (with collaborators)
- Metabolomics and lipidomics (prostaglandins, sphingolipids and ceramides, glucose/PPP metabolism) with clinical mass-spectrometry collaborators
- Human LAM tissue and biofluids through the Cincinnati rare-lung-disease program (McCormack, Gupta): serum prostaglandins, exhaled breath condensate, candidate blood biomarkers
- Single-cell RNA-seq, snATAC-seq and spatial transcriptomics of human LAM lungs (with Yan Xu); Connectivity Map analysis of scRNA-seq signatures

## Representative publications
- Yu J, Astrinidis A, Henske EP. Chromosome 16 loss of heterozygosity in tuberous sclerosis and sporadic lymphangiomyomatosis. Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2001;164:1537-40 (citation from UC directory; PMID not retrieved). Her start in TSC2 two-hit genetics.
- Yu JJ, Robb VA, Morrison TA, ..., Walker CL, Henske EP. Estrogen promotes the survival and pulmonary metastasis of tuberin-null cells. PNAS 2009. PMID 19202070. E2 increases lung metastases 3- to 5-fold and raises circulating tumor cells. This is the foundation of her hormone-driven metastasis model.
- Li C, Lee PS, Sun Y, ..., Kwiatkowski D, Yu JJ. Estradiol and mTORC2 cooperate to enhance prostaglandin biosynthesis and tumorigenesis in TSC2-deficient LAM cells. J Exp Med 2014. PMID 24395886. Rapamycin does not suppress COX-2, but Torin 1 and Rictor knockdown do. Celecoxib and aspirin reduce tumors, and LAM patients have higher serum prostaglandins. A model for her mTORC1-versus-mTORC2 dissection plus patient data.
- Sun Y, Gu X, Zhang E, ..., Henske EP, Yu JJ. Estradiol promotes pentose phosphate pathway addiction and cell survival via reactivation of Akt in mTORC1 hyperactive cells. Cell Death Dis 2014. PMID 24832603. Metabolic dependence as a vulnerability.

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values target validation along a ladder from human tissue to cells to animals. Her papers usually go from human LAM single-cell or patient data, to genetic knockdown and pharmacologic inhibition in TSC2-null cells with TSC2-re-expression controls, to xenograft and/or Tsc2+/- mouse kidney tumor models, often with a rapamycin combination arm. She will probably reward aims that show the same.
- Values separating mTORC1 from mTORC2 and rapamycin-sensitive from rapamycin-insensitive effects. For any aim invoking mTOR she is likely to ask whether rapamycin versus Torin or Rictor-loss experiments distinguish the complexes. She is also likely to ask whether the proposed target stays active under clinical mTOR inhibition.
- Values sex hormones as real experimental variables. Her central model is estrogen-driven. She is likely to notice whether an aim on cystic or tumor kidney disease considers sex differences, hormonal status, and both sexes of animals.
- Scrutinizes TSC versus PKD conflation. Her kidney collaborations show that TSC cysts arise from A-intercalated cells with FOXI1/H+-ATPase/ClC-5 machinery, not CFTR-dependent principal-cell cysts as in PKD. An aim that extrapolates ADPKD mechanisms to TSC kidney disease, or the reverse, would probably get questions on cell of origin and transport mechanism.
- Scrutinizes model fidelity. She works with rat ELT3 cells, MEFs, patient-derived cells and xenografts, so she knows their limits. She may ask how a cell line or xenograft result will be checked in a genetic model or in human tissue.
- Likely enthusiastic about rare-disease translation (drug repurposing, combinations with sirolimus, blood biomarkers) and single-cell or spatial atlases paired with perturbation.
- Outsider areas: renal transport physiology and hemodynamics, nephron development, cilia and channel biophysics, transplantation, dialysis, clinical trial design in CKD. She would read these as an informed cell and cancer biologist, focusing on mechanism, target validation and model choice. She is a pulmonary/TSC scientist, so her kidney expertise is narrow: TSC kidney tumors and cysts.
- Likely questions at the table: "Is this effect mTORC1-dependent, and does it persist under rapamycin?" "What cell type do the cysts come from in your model?" "Have you tested both sexes, and does hormonal status matter?" "Does the cell-line result hold in a genetic model and in human tissue?" "Is the target druggable, and is there a combination rationale with sirolimus?"
