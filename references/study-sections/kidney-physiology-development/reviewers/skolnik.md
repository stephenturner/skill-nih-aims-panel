## Research focus
Skolnik is a nephrologist-turned-signal-transduction biologist. His lab's main question is how the Ca2+-activated K+ channel KCa3.1 (KCNN4) is regulated and what it does. His group showed that KCa3.1 needs PI(3)P generated by the class II PI3K-C2β, plus histidine phosphorylation of His358 by the histidine kinase NDPK-B, to open. MTMR6, PHPT-1 and PGAM5 reverse this. He uses this pathway to probe the little-studied field of mammalian histidine phosphorylation in CD4 T cells, mast cells, β-cells and vascular smooth muscle. On the kidney side he works on ADPKD: KCa3.1-dependent CFTR chloride secretion and cyst growth, glutamine metabolism/GLS1, and unbiased kinome screens for drug targets, tested in Pkd1 conditional mouse models. He also studies TRPV5 regulation by histidine phosphorylation in distal Ca2+ reabsorption. Since about 2023 he has been part of the NYU pig-to-human kidney xenotransplant team (decedent physiology/immunology, clinical trial).

## Model systems and methods
- Patch clamp electrophysiology (inside-out patches, whole-cell) of KCa3.1, TRPV5 and KATP channels; Ussing-chamber anion secretion in monolayers
- Primary human ADPKD cyst-lining and normal human kidney (NHK) epithelial cells; forskolin-induced 3D cyst growth in collagen gels; MDCK cells
- ADPKD mouse models: Aqp2-Cre;Pkd1fl/fl (collecting duct), Pkhd1-Cre;Pkd1fl/fl, and "rapid and slow" models; CRISPR/Cas9 in vivo gene deletion (DDR1)
- Pharmacology in vivo: TRAM-34 (KCa3.1 inhibitor), CB-839/BPTES (GLS1 inhibitors)
- Mass-spectrometry kinome profiling to find kinases upregulated in PKD kidneys
- Knockout mice: KCa3.1-/-, NDPK-B-/-, PHPT-1-/-, PI3KC2β-/-, TRIM27-/-; T cell transfer colitis (rag2-/-), TNBS colitis, passive cutaneous/systemic anaphylaxis, delayed-type hypersensitivity
- Phosphohistidine biochemistry with 1-pHis/3-pHis monoclonal antibodies (with Tony Hunter); ubiquitination assays; siRNA/shRNA
- Metabolic phenotyping: urinary Ca2+ on high-Ca diets, insulin secretion/hypoglycemia
- Human decedent xenotransplant: serial biopsies, multi-omics (with the NYU Transplant Institute)

## Likely reviewing lens (inferred)
- Values: mechanistic signaling with genetic epistasis, i.e., knockouts that phenocopy one another to place molecules in a pathway (NDPK-B-/- phenocopying KCa3.1-/-), plus direct electrophysiological readout. He will probably favor aims with a defined molecular mechanism over descriptive omics.
- Values: drug-target thinking for ADPKD. His R01 is framed as "new therapeutic targets," and he tests pharmacologic inhibitors (TRAM-34, CB-839) in vivo. He is likely to reward aims with a clear druggable node and a path to testing it.
- Scrutinizes (from his own GLS1 and DDR1 experience): single-model PKD claims. He has seen a drug work in Aqp2-Cre;Pkd1 but not Pkhd1-Cre;Pkd1 mice, and an upregulated kinase prove irrelevant on deletion. He will likely ask whether a PKD target is validated in more than one orthologous model (rapid vs. slow, different Cre drivers), whether "upregulated" has been shown to be causal, and whether compensatory pathways were considered.
- Scrutinizes: ion-channel claims without patch-clamp or transport (Ussing) data; specificity of channel pharmacology (e.g., TRAM-34 vs. genetic loss); PI3K-isoform specificity; antibody-based phosphohistidine claims (pHis is acid-labile, so methods matter).
- Scrutinizes: in cyst-fluid-secretion aims, whether the electrochemical driving force (basolateral K+ channels) and apical CFTR are both addressed. Distal Ca2+ handling and TRPV5 regulation are within his expertise.
- Outsider: kidney development/nephrogenesis, glomerular/podocyte biology, neural control of the kidney/blood pressure, epidemiology and large clinical trials, dialysis access. He would read these as a broadly trained clinician-scientist, mostly judging mechanistic logic and rigor.
- Likely questions at the table: "Is the target causal or just upregulated?" "Has this been tested in a second PKD model?" "Where is the direct channel/transport measurement?" "How specific is the inhibitor, and is there genetic confirmation?" "Is there a druggable step, and is it translatable to patients?"
