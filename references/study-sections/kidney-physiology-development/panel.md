## Official scope (CSR, last updated 10/16/2026)

"The Kidney Physiology and Development study section reviews grant applications that focus on the developmental mechanisms and function of the kidney. Studies focus on basic and applied aspects of normal renal physiology, transport biology, obstructive diseases affecting the kidney, and renal replacement including transplantation and hemodialysis."

Topics:
- Kidney epithelial cell biology, including mechanisms of renal transport systems; hormonal and neural regulation of renal function; and other processes relevant to normal renal physiology.
- Kidney transplantation and renal replacement therapies, including basic and clinical studies of uremia, and including dialysis, kidney ablation, artificial kidneys, chronic allograft nephropathy, and prevention and/or treatment of rejection.
- Primary congenital and acquired kidney conditions affecting the kidney, including development, epidemiology, diagnosis and management.
- Polycystic kidney disease, including ciliopathies and ciliary structure of the kidney tubules, and genetic models of polycystic kidney disease.

Boundaries (who gets what):
- PBKD takes injury from hypertension, renovascular disease or specific disease states, and genetic mechanisms of specific kidney diseases. KURS(82) takes normal fluid/electrolyte transport and developmental genetics.
- KEDD takes determinants, predictors and biomarkers of kidney conditions in human subpopulations (epidemiology). KURS(82) takes developmental mechanisms and function in humans.
- ITA takes immunological mechanisms and treatment of rejection. KURS(82) takes organ transport, preservation, viability, ischemia/reperfusion.
- KURS(83) takes ureter, bladder, urethra, male genital tract, pelvic floor. KURS(82) takes kidney function, stones, development, kidney-specific mechanisms.
- General cilia biology goes to the Cell and Developmental Biology branch; PKD-focused cilia work comes here.
- Renal cell carcinoma is excluded.

Use these boundaries in reviews. An aims page that reads like PBKD (disease injury mechanism), KEDD (population biomarkers) or ITA (rejection immunology) may be assigned elsewhere or, if it lands here, read by a room whose center of gravity is physiology and development. Say so when it applies.

## Cluster archetypes (for "room" agents)

Use these when spawning agents to represent the unassigned members who will read the aims page and vote. Each archetype agent reads the profiles of its members and speaks for their shared lens.

- A. Developmental and molecular nephrology (Park, Rauchman, Kreidberg, Lin). Genetic rigor: Cre specificity and timing, lineage tracing for fate claims, direct-target evidence (ChIP/CUT&RUN) for transcription-factor claims, separation-of-function alleles, rigorous nephron counting, functional phenotype beyond marker changes, human tissue or organoid anchoring.
- B. Cilia, polycystins and channel cell biology (Delling, Tsiokas, Skolnik, Barr). Direct measurement of channel activity where the claim is made (ciliary patch clamp, targeted indicators), localization at adequate resolution, rescue and epistasis experiments, more than one PKD model, causal rather than "upregulated" targets. Strong disagreement inside this cluster on cilia as flow sensors (see fault lines); Barr's work frames polycystins as a sensory complex, so she is likely more open to sensing premises than Delling but will want genetic tests.
- C. Clinical and translational PKD (Dell, Irazabal, both standing; Yu tentatively, as the TSC/mTOR cystic-disease voice). Validated progression readouts (beyond TKV/eGFR), model fidelity to the human disease (ARPKD vs ADPKD, genetic background, disease stage), both sexes, rodent-to-patient continuity, power for rare disease.
- D. Transport and integrative physiology (Pastor-Soler, Monu, Stocker; Wall and Arroyo Ornelas provisional). Functional readouts (current, flux, micropuncture, SNA, telemetry) over western blots and imaging; surface versus total protein; pharmacology confirmed genetically; preparation and anesthesia stated; stimulus specificity.
- E. Clinical nephrology, trials and CKD complications (Kendrick, Gamboa, Roy-Chaudhury; two standing). Pre-specified powered endpoints, target engagement, realistic effect sizes, confounding control, uremia actually modeled, path to patients, interdependence of aims.
- F. Transplantation (Amaral, Panzer, Mas). Human biopsy or cohort validation, independent validation cohorts for biomarkers, blinded Banff scoring, models that develop chronic lesions, pediatric and equity considerations, clinical endpoint timing.

## Panel-level dynamics (inferred from the roster and profiles)

- The room is mixed basic and clinical, roughly half each. Whatever the topic, most voters will be outsiders to it. Aims pages that only a specialist can parse lose the unassigned votes even when the assigned reviewers like them.
- PKD and cilia are over-represented: Delling, Tsiokas, Skolnik, Dell and Irazabal, plus Pastor-Soler and Kreidberg with PKD side projects. PKD applications get expert, demanding reads from several angles at once (channel biophysics, cell biology, clinical imaging).
- Developmental biology has four senior, genetically rigorous members. Expect Cre-driver and lineage-tracing scrutiny on any development aim.
- Three transplant members span epidemiology, immunology and genomics, but rejection immunology is formally ITA's territory. Transplant aims fit here best when framed around organ quality, preservation, ischemia/reperfusion, repair or long-term function.
- The chair ran KUFD and is a clinical trialist. Expect discussion to be pushed toward concrete weaknesses and away from topic preferences, and for "if Aim 1 fails, do Aims 2 and 3 survive?" to come up.
- Rigor themes nearly everyone shares: sex as a biological variable (explicit in at least eight profiles), genetic confirmation of pharmacology, direct functional measurement, and human relevance.

## Known fault lines (where pleasing one reviewer can cost another)

- Cilia as flow sensors. Delling published that primary cilia are not calcium-responsive mechanosensors. Tsiokas and Pastor-Soler work within ciliary signaling models. An aim premised on "cilia sense flow through polycystin Ca2+ influx" draws a premise objection from Delling. Safer framing: state the claim as a testable hypothesis, cite the negative data, and use measurements that can distinguish ciliary from cytoplasmic signals.
- Mechanism depth versus translational path. Clusters A, B and D reward molecular depth; C, E and F reward a line to patients. The best-scoring pages do both in one sentence each: a named mechanism and a named clinical consequence.
- Mouse versus rat versus human. Monu and Stocker treat rat physiology as the standard for hemodynamics; developmental biologists work in mouse; Roy-Chaudhury asks for large-animal or human tissue. Justify the species choice in the aims page itself when the claim is physiological.
- Single-cell atlases. Mas and Park value them; Rauchman and Park both press "describe versus perturb." Pair any atlas aim with a perturbation.
